Program

Two consecutive 90-minute sessions, each corresponding to a stage of deployment.

David L. Lawrence Convention Center, Pittsburgh, USA · date TBC

Session 1 · 90 min

Research Deployment

How technologies are introduced into everyday care settings, how researchers work with older adults and caregivers during deployment, and how outcomes are evaluated over time.

  1. 20 min
    Multi-stakeholder panel

    Speakers from academia, industry, and clinical or care settings describe current projects and discuss how deployment priorities differ across sectors: goals, timelines, staffing, definitions of success, regulatory requirements, existing workflows, and who maintains the technology over time. Confirmed panelists: panelists TBC.

  2. 20 min
    Poster session

    Authors present experiences with real-world deployments: challenges faced, lessons learned, unexpected outcomes, and approaches to long-term evaluation.

  3. 20 min
    Breakout discussions

    Facilitated groups organized around the first three workshop themes, working from guiding questions and participants' own deployment experiences.

  4. 30 min
    Full-group discussion

    Groups report the main challenges, practices, and open questions they identified, followed by discussion of issues cutting across all three themes.

Session 2 · 90 min

Translation and Scale

What is needed to move beyond an individual research deployment and support use across other settings and organizations.

  1. 15 min
    Poster session

    Posters on translation, adaptation, implementation, or scaling, including the challenges encountered along the way.

  2. 15 min
    Breakout discussions

    What makes it difficult to move a promising research deployment beyond the original study: partnerships, opportunities for continued use, adaptation to new settings, and the move from research goals toward implementation.

  3. 45 min
    Full-group discussion

    How academia, industry, clinical and care organizations, community partners, older adults, and caregivers can work across differing goals, timelines, resources, and definitions of success. What evidence each stakeholder needs before moving a technology forward, how responsibility for maintenance, support, data, and risk is shared, and which lessons transfer between settings.

  4. 15 min
    Collective reflection and next steps

    Reviewing the main points from both sessions and identifying priorities for future work on conducting, evaluating, and translating real-world deployments.
