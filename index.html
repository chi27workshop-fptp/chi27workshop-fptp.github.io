From Prototype to Practice

Deploying and bringing technologies into real-world use for older adults and their caregivers

David L. Lawrence Convention Center, Pittsburgh, USA · date TBD

Call for Participation View the Program

About

Laboratory and controlled studies help researchers understand the usability, accessibility, and feasibility of technologies for older adults and their caregivers. Once these technologies move into homes, care settings, and other everyday environments, their use becomes intertwined with routines, relationships, physical environments, organizational practices, and available support.

Real-world deployments introduce technical, logistical, relational, and organizational challenges that can continue well beyond installation. Evaluation also becomes more complex as needs, care arrangements, and expectations change over time, raising questions about what to evaluate, when to evaluate it, and whose perspectives should inform that evaluation.

This workshop focuses on two related questions:

  • How can we responsibly conduct and evaluate real-world technology deployments as support needs, relationships, and care arrangements differ significantly?
  • What resources and knowledge are needed to move a promising technology deployment beyond an individual research study toward continued and sustained use across care settings and institutions?

The workshop will consist of two consecutive 90-minute sessions.

Themes

Theme 1Conducting and Evaluating Real-World Technology Deployments

  • Moving prototypes into everyday care settings: installation, onboarding, training, accessibility, interoperability, technical support, and integration with household routines, care practices, and organizational workflows.
  • Relationships, trust, and participant experience: communication, changing expectations, repeated interactions with research teams, sensitive data collection, consent, and changes in health or care arrangements.
  • Evaluating whether a technology is “working”: considering usability and reliability alongside continued use, autonomy, caregiver burden, well-being, social connection, and relationships.

Theme 2Moving Promising Deployments Beyond a Single Research Study

  • Building cross-sector partnerships: collaboration across academia, industry, clinical and care institutions, community partners, older adults, and caregivers, including differences in goals, timelines, resources, responsibilities, and definitions of success.
  • Translating deployment lessons across settings: understanding what can transfer, what needs to be adapted, and how deployment experiences can inform future design, implementation, and continued use.

Call for Participation

Research on technologies for older adults and caregivers begins with design and early-stage evaluation. Real-world deployments allow researchers to understand how technologies are used over time and within care practices. However, these deployments require technical support, coordination with older adults and caregivers, and responses to changing needs, routines, and expectations. Evaluating success can be difficult when outcomes include usability, independence, caregiver burden, well-being, and continued use. This workshop focuses on two areas: conducting and evaluating real-world deployments, and considering how promising deployments can move beyond an individual study toward continued use across settings.

We invite researchers, students, and practitioners interested in technologies for older adults and caregivers.

Submission options:

  1. Position paper – 2–4 pages, excluding references, following the ACM Extended Abstract format.
  2. Pictorial
  3. Statement of interest – 250–500 words.

Submissions should engage with one or both workshop themes and may describe deployment experiences, methodological reflections, or efforts to sustain deployments in the long term.

Review process: submissions will be reviewed by at least two workshop organizers and selected based on relevance to the workshop goals and the perspectives they contribute.

If accepted:

  • Authors of accepted position papers will present lightning talks.
  • Papers will be made publicly available through arXiv and the workshop website.
